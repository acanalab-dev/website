Positive affirmations for kids: 50 simple phrases

Positive affirmations for kids are short, simple sentences that help children build a kind and confident inner voice. Here are 50, grouped by age and situation, with easy ways to teach them and fun activities to try at home or in class.

Why positive affirmations matter for kids

Children are constantly forming a story about who they are. "I'm bad at maths." "Nobody wants to play with me." "I'm brave." Those small sentences, repeated in their heads, shape how they approach the next challenge.

Positive affirmations for kids are short, simple sentences that give them a kinder, more helpful story to repeat: "I can do hard things", "Mistakes help me learn", "I am loved". Said together with a parent or teacher, they also become a warm little ritual, which is often what kids remember most.

The key with children is to keep it simple and concrete. Short words, things they can picture, and nothing that sounds like pressure. "I am the best at everything" sets them up to feel like a failure. "I try my best, and that's enough" doesn't.

10 affirmations for little kids (ages 3 to 6)

Very short, easy to repeat, and ideally said together with a hug or a high five.

  1. I am loved.
  2. I am kind.
  3. I am brave.
  4. I can try new things.
  5. I am a good friend.
  6. I can ask for help.
  7. My feelings are okay.
  8. I am safe.
  9. I am special just as I am.
  10. I can do it!

10 affirmations for school and learning

  1. I can do hard things.
  2. Mistakes help me learn.
  3. I am a good learner.
  4. It's okay not to know something yet.
  5. I keep trying, even when it's tricky.
  6. I can ask my teacher a question.
  7. My brain grows when I practice.
  8. I am proud of my effort.
  9. I can focus on one thing at a time.
  10. Every day I learn something new.

10 affirmations for confidence and friendship

  1. I like who I am.
  2. I am a good friend, and I deserve good friends.
  3. I can say hello to someone new.
  4. It's okay to be different. Different is interesting.
  5. I can say no when something doesn't feel right.
  6. I don't have to be perfect to be liked.
  7. I can share, and I can take turns.
  8. My ideas matter.
  9. I stand up for myself and for others.
  10. I am brave, even when I feel a little scared.

10 affirmations for big feelings

For tears, tantrums, worries and frustration. Say these calmly after the storm has passed, or while breathing together.

  1. All my feelings are allowed.
  2. I can take a deep breath and calm my body.
  3. Big feelings come and go.
  4. I can use my words to say how I feel.
  5. It's okay to cry.
  6. I can ask for a hug.
  7. When I'm angry, I can take a break.
  8. I am still loved, even when I make a mistake.
  9. I can start again.
  10. I am safe, and this feeling will pass.

10 affirmations for older kids and teens

Older kids often find "cute" affirmations embarrassing. These are more grown-up, and work well written in a notebook or as a phone wallpaper.

  1. I am allowed to take up space.
  2. My worth isn't measured by likes or grades.
  3. I can handle setbacks and keep going.
  4. I'm still figuring things out, and that's normal.
  5. I choose friends who treat me well.
  6. I can make my own choices and learn from them.
  7. I don't have to compare myself to anyone.
  8. My voice deserves to be heard.
  9. I am becoming who I want to be.
  10. I am enough, right now.

How to teach affirmations to kids

  1. Say them together. Kids copy what they see. Saying an affirmation yourself ("I can be patient today") shows them it's a normal thing to do.
  2. Attach them to a routine. The school run, brushing teeth or bedtime. A fixed moment turns it into a habit without nagging.
  3. Let them choose. Offer three and let your child pick their favorite. Kids are much more likely to repeat a sentence they chose.
  4. Connect them to real moments. When they finish a hard puzzle, remind them: "See? You can do hard things." That makes the words feel true.
  5. Keep it light. Two or three affirmations, under a minute. If they're not in the mood, skip it today.

4 fun affirmation activities for kids

  • Affirmation jar. Write 30 affirmations on slips of paper and let your child pull one out each morning. That's a month of different sentences, and you can refill it with their own ideas.
  • Mirror talk. Stand in front of the mirror together and say an affirmation with a big smile. Silly voices encouraged.
  • Affirmation cards. Let your child decorate index cards with their favorite affirmations and keep them in a backpack or lunchbox.
  • Bedtime three. At bedtime, each of you says one thing you did well today and one affirmation for tomorrow. Our guide to affirmations for sleep has a short list of bedtime affirmations for kids too.
Praise effort, not just results. "You kept trying" backs up "I can do hard things" far better than "You're so smart".

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Don't use affirmations to dismiss feelings. If a child is sad, acknowledge it first ("That was really disappointing") before offering a helpful sentence.
  • Make them believable. Choose affirmations about effort and growth over ones about being "the best". Our guide on how to write affirmations explains why.
  • Model self-kindness. Kids notice how you talk about yourself. A few self-love affirmations for you are a good example for them.
  • Ask for help when it's more than a phase. Affirmations are a nice everyday habit. If a child's worries or low mood last for weeks, talk to their teacher, school counselor or doctor.

Frequently asked questions

What are positive affirmations for kids?

Short, simple sentences children repeat to themselves, such as "I can do hard things" or "Mistakes help me learn". They help kids build a kinder, more confident way of talking to themselves.

At what age can kids start using affirmations?

Even preschoolers can say very short ones like "I am brave" or "I am loved" together with a parent. From around school age, kids can choose their own, and teens often prefer writing them down privately.

When is the best time to say affirmations with kids?

Attach them to something you already do every day: the school run, breakfast or bedtime. One minute is plenty.

How do I get my child to say affirmations?

Do it together, keep it playful and let them choose. An affirmation jar, mirror talk or decorated cards make it feel like a game rather than a task.

Can affirmations help a child with anxiety?

They can be a comforting everyday habit, especially paired with slow breathing. They aren't a treatment, so if worries last for weeks or get in the way of daily life, talk to their teacher, school counselor or doctor.
