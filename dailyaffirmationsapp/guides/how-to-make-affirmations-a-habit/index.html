How to make affirmations a habit: 7 steps that stick

Affirmations only help if you keep using them, and that's where most people stop. Here's how to make affirmations a habit in 7 simple steps, how long it really takes, and 20 affirmations to start with.

Why affirmations are easy to start and hard to keep

Most people who try affirmations have the same experience. The first few days feel good. Then a busy morning comes, you skip a day, then another, and a week later you've forgotten about it. Nothing went wrong with the affirmations. They just never became a habit.

That matters, because any effect affirmations have comes from repetition. One kind sentence on one morning is nice. The same kind of sentence most mornings for a few months slowly changes how you talk to yourself. Our guide do affirmations work? explains what research says about that.

The good news is that an affirmation habit is one of the easiest habits to build. It takes a minute, needs no equipment and can be done anywhere. Here's how to make it stick.

How long does it take to build the habit?

You may have heard that a habit takes 21 days. That number is a myth. In a well-known 2009 study from University College London, people took anywhere from 18 to 254 days to make a new daily behavior automatic, with a median of about 66 days. Simple behaviors, like drinking a glass of water, became automatic faster than harder ones.

Two other findings from that study are useful. Missing a single day didn't meaningfully set people back. And the behavior became automatic most reliably when it was done in the same situation every day. So aim for consistency, not perfection, and think in months rather than weeks.

7 steps to make affirmations a daily habit

  1. Start tiny. Commit to one affirmation, said once. That's the whole habit at first. You can always do more on good days, but the minimum should be so small that you can do it on your worst day.
  2. Attach it to something you already do. This is called habit stacking. Pick a daily anchor and say your affirmation right after it: "After I pour my coffee, I say my affirmation." Brushing your teeth, unlocking your phone for the first time or sitting down at your desk all work.
  3. Make it visible. Put your affirmation where your anchor happens. A sticky note on the mirror, a card next to the kettle or a widget on your Home Screen means you don't have to remember to look for it.
  4. Choose words you believe. If an affirmation feels false, you'll quietly stop saying it. "I'm learning to trust myself" lasts longer than "I'm fearless". Our guide on how to write affirmations shows how to adjust the wording.
  5. Use a reminder for the first few weeks. A single daily notification at the same time works as training wheels until the habit runs on its own.
  6. Track your streak. A simple tick on a calendar or a streak counter in an app makes progress visible. Seeing ten days in a row is a surprisingly strong reason not to skip day eleven.
  7. Plan for missed days. Decide now what happens when you miss one: you simply do it the next day. The rule many people find helpful is "never miss twice".
Write your plan as one sentence and put it where you'll see it: "After I [anchor], I will say [affirmation]." Being this specific is one of the simplest ways to turn an intention into a habit.

10 affirmations to start your habit with

Short, believable and easy to remember. Pick one and use it for a week before you change it.

  1. I am doing my best, and that is enough.
  2. Today I choose to be kind to myself.
  3. I can handle what today brings.
  4. I am growing a little every day.
  5. I deserve good things.
  6. I trust myself to make good choices.
  7. I am calm, steady and ready.
  8. Small steps still move me forward.
  9. I am proud of who I'm becoming.
  10. I have everything I need to begin.

10 affirmations about consistency itself

These are for the habit, not the day. Use one when you notice motivation dipping.

  1. I show up for myself, even in small ways.
  2. Consistency matters more than perfection.
  3. One minute a day is enough to make a difference.
  4. Missing a day doesn't undo my progress.
  5. I keep the promises I make to myself.
  6. Every day I practice, it gets easier.
  7. I am building habits that support me.
  8. I can start again at any moment.
  9. My small daily choices add up.
  10. I am becoming someone who follows through.

Common mistakes that break the habit

  • Doing too much at once. A list of fifteen affirmations, journaling and meditation all at once feels great for three days and then collapses. Start with one sentence.
  • No fixed time or place. "Sometime during the day" usually means never. Tie it to a specific anchor.
  • Saying the same words forever. After a few weeks, a sentence can stop registering. Keep the habit, but refresh the words every week or two.
  • Treating a missed day as failure. One missed day doesn't matter. Giving up because of it does.
  • Expecting quick results. Affirmations work slowly, like most habits. Judge them after a couple of months, not after a week.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to make affirmations a habit?

It varies. A well-known study found new daily habits took between 18 and 254 days to become automatic, with a median of about 66. Simple habits like a one-sentence affirmation tend to form faster.

What is the best time of day to say affirmations?

The best time is one you can repeat every day. Mornings are the most popular, but any fixed moment works, like brushing your teeth, your first coffee or going to bed.

How many affirmations should I say a day?

Start with one. Once the habit is steady, you can add a few more, but a single believable affirmation said every day beats a long list you skip.

What if I miss a day?

Just do it the next day. Missing one day doesn't undo your progress. The helpful rule is "never miss twice".

Do I need an app to build an affirmation habit?

No. A sticky note and a calendar you tick off work well. An app helps by handling the reminder, the streak and fresh affirmations for you.
