Streaming, music, cloud storage, a gym app, a meal kit: subscriptions pile up quietly, and in a couple they're often spread across two cards with no clear rule about who pays for what. Each one is small, so nobody wants to make a fuss, but together they can add up to a real monthly amount. This guide walks through listing them, deciding which are shared, choosing a simple way to split them, and handling yearly plans and family plans, with a worked example.

Step 1: List every subscription

Most people can name three or four subscriptions off the top of their head and have more than that. Go through both of your bank statements and app store purchase lists for the last two or three months, and write down each one:

  • Name and price, per month or per year.
  • Whose card pays for it.
  • Who uses it: one of you, both, or nobody lately.
  • Renewal date for anything paid yearly.

Common ones to check: video and music streaming, cloud storage, phone plans, news and magazines, fitness apps and gyms, software, meal kits, and delivery memberships.

Tip: look in your phone's subscription settings as well as your bank statement. Some subscriptions are billed through the app store and show up under a different name.

Step 2: Decide which ones are shared

A simple rule works for most couples: if you both use it regularly, it's shared. If one of you uses it, it's personal.

  • Usually shared: the streaming service you watch together, home internet, a family music or cloud plan you both use.
  • Usually personal: your own gym, a gaming subscription only one of you plays, a magazine only one of you reads.
  • Grey areas: a service one of you uses a lot and the other occasionally. Agree once: shared, personal, or shared at a different ratio.

While you're at it, mark anything neither of you has used in the last month or two. That's worth a quick conversation about whether to keep it.

Step 3: Pick a way to split them

There are three simple approaches. Pick one and stick with it.

Split each one and settle up

Whoever's card pays keeps paying. You add the shared subscriptions to your shared costs and settle the difference with everything else at the end of the month. It's the most exact option.

Take turns paying

Each of you pays for some of the shared subscriptions, so the totals come out roughly even. “I pay the streaming, you pay the music and the cloud storage.” It's simple and needs no transfers, as long as the totals stay close.

Pay from a joint account

If you already have a joint account for shared costs, move the shared subscriptions there. They come out of the money you both pay in, and there's nothing to settle. Our joint vs separate accounts guide compares the setups.

If you split other costs by income, you can use the same ratio for subscriptions, but for small amounts many couples just go 50/50 to keep it simple.

Step 4: A worked example

Ema and Filip list their subscriptions:

Video streaming€15.99EmaYes

Music family plan€17.99EmaYes
Cloud storage, shared plan€2.99FilipYes
Ema's gym€35.00EmaNo
Filip's gaming subscription€10.00FilipNo

The shared subscriptions come to €15.99 + €9.99 + €17.99 + €2.99 = €46.96 a month. Ema pays €33.98 of that and Filip pays €12.98.

At 50/50, each share is €46.96 ÷ 2 = €23.48. Ema pays €10.50 more than her share, so Filip owes Ema €10.50 a month. The gym and the gaming subscription stay out of it.

They could add the €10.50 to their monthly settle-up. Or they could take turns instead: if Filip took over the music plan, he'd pay €30.97 and Ema €15.99. That swings the other way, almost €15 apart, so for them settling up is fairer than swapping. Our guide on how to settle up who owes what walks through the method.

Step 5: Yearly plans, family plans and free trials

  • Yearly plans: split the cost when it's paid. A €120 yearly plan at 50/50 is €60 each. If you'd rather keep monthly numbers even, think of it as €10 a month.
  • Family plans: agree who is the account holder, since that person usually controls the plan and its payment. Write down who's on it.
  • Free trials: decide before the trial ends whether it becomes shared, personal, or cancelled. Otherwise it quietly turns into a cost nobody chose.
  • Price changes: subscriptions sometimes get more expensive. When one does, check that your split still makes sense.

Step 6: Review a few times a year

Subscriptions change more often than rent or bills. Every three or four months, go through the list together:

  1. Is everything still used?
  2. Has anything new appeared, or a price changed?
  3. Is anything shared that's now really personal, or the other way round?
  4. Does your split still feel fair?

It takes ten minutes, and it fits well into a monthly money talk. The guide on how to split bills with your partner covers the bigger shared costs in the same way.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Ignoring small amounts. €5 here and €10 there can add up to more than you expect.
  • Forgetting yearly renewals. They're the ones that surprise you.
  • Sharing everything by default. Personal subscriptions are fine; just keep them out of the shared total.
  • No clear account holder. Know who controls each shared plan, in case something changes.
