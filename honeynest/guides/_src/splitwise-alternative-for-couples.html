Many couples start sharing costs with a general expense-splitting app, and that works well for lots of people. Others find that what two people living together need is a bit different from what a group of friends on a trip needs. This guide covers what couples usually need from a shared expenses app, a worked example of how the numbers should look, a short and factual note on Splitwise, and an honest look at when each kind of app may suit you.

What couples usually need from a shared expenses app

Before comparing apps, it helps to list what you actually want. For most couples, it comes down to a few things:

  • Two people, not groups. You share one household, so you don't need to manage lots of groups or invite friends.
  • Who paid what. Every shared cost should show which of you paid it.
  • Running totals. Each partner's total, their share in percent, and the difference, without doing the math yourself.
  • Shared and personal kept apart. Rent and groceries are shared; your own hobbies usually aren't.
  • Recurring costs. Rent, bills and subscriptions come every month, so you shouldn't have to type them in every time.
  • Both of you can add costs. If only one person can log expenses, the record is always half finished.
  • A split rule you both agreed on. 50/50 or by income. The app should support the rule you chose, not the other way round. The guide to splitting expenses as a couple compares the common methods.
Tip: write your must-haves down before you try any app. It's easy to be impressed by features you'll never use and miss the one you need every week.

Couple apps and group apps solve different problems

Group expense apps are built for many people sharing costs in many combinations: four friends on a ski trip, five roommates, a stag weekend. That flexibility is very useful when the group keeps changing.

A couple's situation is usually simpler and more steady. It's the same two people, much the same costs every month, and one ongoing balance. What matters most is that the record is quick to keep up and that you both see the same numbers. Neither kind of app is right or wrong. It depends on whether most of your shared spending is with one partner or with many different people.

A worked example: one month of shared costs

Here's what a month looks like for Sara and Luka, who split shared costs 50/50:

CostAmountPaid by
Rent€1,100Sara
Groceries€420Sara €180, Luka €240
Electricity and internet€160Luka
Streaming subscriptions€30Sara
Date nights€140Luka
Shared total€1,850Sara €1,310, Luka €540

Sara paid 71% of the total and Luka 29%. At 50/50, each share is €1,850 ÷ 2 = €925. Sara paid €385 more than her share, so Luka sends Sara €385 and they're even.

One thing to watch: the difference between their totals is €1,310 − €540 = €770. That's not what Luka owes. At 50/50 the transfer is half the difference, €385, because the money moves from one share to the other. Many people get this wrong the first time.

A good app shows you the totals, the percentages and the difference at a glance, so this takes a minute instead of an evening with bank statements. The guide on how to settle up who owes what walks through the method for other splits too.

When Splitwise may suit you better

Being honest about this helps you pick the right tool:

  • You share a lot of costs with friends, roommates or groups, not only with your partner.
  • One or both of you doesn't use an iPhone, or you'd like to use a web browser too.
  • You want to split individual expenses unequally, for example by exact amounts or shares.
  • You often spend in foreign currencies and want the app to convert them.

How to choose: five questions

  1. Who do you share costs with? Mostly your partner, or lots of different people?
  2. Which phones do you both use? Some apps are on one platform only.
  3. How do you split? If you split 50/50, almost any app works. If you split by income or per expense, check the app supports it.
  4. Do you spend in several currencies? If often, conversion may matter to you.
  5. How much effort will you really put in? The app you'll actually open every week beats the one with the longest feature list.

Try it for one month

Whichever app you pick, give it a real test:

  1. Agree your split and what counts as shared before you start.
  2. Both of you log every shared cost for one month, at the moment you pay.
  3. At the end of the month, check the totals and settle up once.
  4. Ask each other: was it easy, and do we both trust the numbers?

If the answer is yes, keep going. If not, the problem is usually the habit rather than the app. Our guide on how to track shared expenses has tips that work with any tool.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing an app before agreeing a split. The app records your agreement; it can't make it for you.
  • Only one partner logging costs. The record ends up incomplete and feels one-sided.
  • Sending the whole difference. At 50/50, the transfer is half the difference between your totals.
  • Switching apps every month. Give one a fair try before moving on.
