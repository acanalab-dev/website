Agreeing on a split is the easy part. The harder part is the logistics: who actually pays the landlord, whose card the internet comes off, what to do when the electricity bill doubles in winter, and how to even it out at the end of the month. This guide walks through a bill-by-bill setup that keeps it simple.

Step 1: List every bill once

Sit down together for twenty minutes and write down every regular bill you share. For each one, note four things:

  • Amount: the usual monthly cost, or a rough average if it changes.
  • Due date: when it leaves the account, so nobody is caught short.
  • Fixed or variable: rent and internet stay the same; electricity, heating and groceries don't.
  • Who it's with: whose account or card it's paid from today.

Don't forget the bills that come once or twice a year, like home insurance or an annual subscription. They're the ones that cause surprises.

Tip: go through the last two months of both your bank statements. It's the fastest way to find the bills you forgot.

Step 2: Pick one rule for splitting

Most couples are happiest with one rule for all shared bills, plus a short list of exceptions. Common rules are 50/50, or a split in proportion to what you each earn. Then agree on the exceptions once, so you don't renegotiate them every month. Typical ones: a streaming service only one of you watches, a car only one of you drives, or a pet one of you brought into the relationship.

Step 3: Decide who pays which bill

A split tells you how much each of you should pay. You still need to decide how the money actually moves. There are three common setups.

1. Pay as it comes, settle monthly

Each bill stays wherever it's paid today. You note who paid what, and once a month the person who paid less sends the difference. It needs no new accounts, but it only works if you keep a record.

2. Each takes some bills

One of you pays rent, the other pays utilities, internet and groceries. There's nothing to work out per bill, which many couples like. The catch is that the totals rarely match your split, especially when rent is much bigger than everything else, so you'll usually need a small fixed transfer to balance it.

3. A joint account for bills

You both pay a set amount into a joint account each month, and the shared bills are paid from it. Each person's amount can be equal or proportional. It's tidy once it's set up, but you need to agree what happens if the account runs low or builds up extra.

Step 4: A worked example

Mia and Tom share a flat. Here's one month of their shared bills:

BillAmountPaid by
Rent€950Mia
Electricity€80Tom
Internet€35Tom
Water€30Tom
Streaming€20Mia
Groceries€400Mia €150, Tom €250
Total€1,515Mia €1,120, Tom €395

Setup 1, split 50/50: each person's share is €757.50. Mia paid €1,120, which is €362.50 more than her share, so Tom sends Mia €362.50. Afterwards they've each paid €757.50.

Setup 1, split 55/45 (Mia earns more): Mia's share is €833.25 and Tom's is €681.75. Mia paid €286.75 more than her share, so Tom sends her €286.75.

Setup 2, each takes some bills: Mia keeps rent and streaming (€970). Tom takes electricity, internet, water and all the groceries (€545). That's still €425 apart, so for a 50/50 split Tom sends Mia half of that, €212.50, each month. Both end up at €757.50.

Setup 3, joint account: at 50/50 they'd each put in about €760, a little over €757.50 to leave a small cushion. At 55/45 it would be about €835 from Mia and €685 from Tom.

All three setups give the same result. Pick the one that's least work for the two of you.

Step 5: Handle variable and yearly bills

Variable bills like electricity and heating can swing a lot between seasons. Two simple ways to deal with them:

  • Split the actual amount each month. It's exact, but the settle-up amount changes.
  • Use an average. If electricity runs €55 in summer and €110 in winter, plan around something like €80 a month and correct the difference every few months.

Yearly bills are easiest when you spread them out. A €240 yearly home insurance bill is €20 a month, or €10 each at 50/50. Set that aside monthly, or add it to the joint account amount, so the yearly payment isn't a shock for whoever's card it hits.

Step 6: When a bill is in one person's name

It's common for the lease, the energy contract or the internet to be in only one name. Who's on the account and who pays a share are two separate questions, so agree on both:

  • Make sure both of you can see the bill, for example by forwarding it or sharing it each month.
  • Agree who's responsible for paying it on time, and who handles the provider if something goes wrong.
  • If you want to change whose name a contract or lease is in, ask the provider or landlord what that involves where you live.

Step 7: A ten-minute monthly check

Pick a fixed day, like the first Sunday of the month, and go through the same short list:

  1. Check that every bill was paid, and add any new or changed ones.
  2. Look at the total shared spending and what each of you paid.
  3. Work out the difference from your split and settle it with one transfer.
  4. Every few months, ask whether the split still feels fair, especially after a raise, a new job or a move.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting the yearly bills. They're the ones that make one person feel they always pay more.
  • Settling every bill separately. Ten small transfers a month are easy to miss. One monthly difference is easier.
  • Assigning bills and never checking the totals. Bills grow at different speeds, so a fair split today can drift.
  • Keeping the record in one person's head. If only one of you knows what was paid, the other can't check it. Keep it somewhere you can both see.
