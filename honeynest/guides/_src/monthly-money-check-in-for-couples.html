Most money arguments between couples aren't about one big decision. They build up from small things nobody mentioned: a bill one of you always pays, a goal that quietly stalled, a cost that came as a surprise. A short monthly money check-in clears those up before they grow. This guide gives you a ready-to-use template, a worked example with real numbers, and ideas for keeping it short and pleasant.

Why once a month works

Once a month lines up with how most money moves: salaries, rent, bills and subscriptions. It's often enough that nothing gets out of hand, and rare enough that it doesn't feel like a chore. Twenty minutes is usually plenty.

If you've never really talked about money together, start with one longer conversation first. Our guide on how to talk about money with your partner covers how to have it without blame. The monthly check-in is what keeps things on track afterwards.

Step 1: Five minutes of prep

A check-in is much quicker when the numbers are ready. Before you sit down, one of you (or each of you, taking turns) gathers:

  • Total shared spending for the month, and who paid what.
  • Your planned amount for shared costs, if you have a budget.
  • Progress on any shared goals.
  • Anything big coming up in the next month or two.

If you already keep a shared record of expenses, this takes a few minutes. If not, our guide on how to track shared expenses shows simple ways to start.

Tip: put the check-in in both your calendars on a fixed day, for example the first Sunday after payday. A set date means nobody has to bring it up.

Step 2: The 20-minute template

Go through these six points in order. The times are a guide, not a rule.

PartQuestionTime
1. Quick winWhat went well with money this month?2 min
2. SpendingHow much did we spend on shared costs, and how does it compare with our plan?5 min
3. Settle upWho paid what, and what transfer evens it out?3 min
4. GoalsAre our shared goals on track?3 min
5. Look aheadWhat's coming up next month or the month after?4 min
6. One changeIs there one thing we want to do differently?3 min

Starting with something that went well sets a friendly tone. Ending with just one change keeps it realistic. A list of ten resolutions rarely survives the month.

Step 3: A worked example

Nika and Sven split shared costs 50/50 and plan for €2,000 a month. Here's their November check-in.

Quick win: they cooked at home more and spent less on takeaways.

Spending: shared costs came to €2,140, so €140 over plan. A quick look shows why: groceries were €90 above usual because of a family dinner they hosted, and the kettle broke (€50). Nothing to worry about.

Settle up:

Amount
Total shared spending€2,140
Paid by Nika€1,320
Paid by Sven€820
Each person's share (50/50)€1,070

Nika paid €250 more than her share, so Sven sends her €250.

Goals: they're saving €150 a month towards a €2,400 holiday. After seven months they have €1,050, exactly on plan. Our guide to saving for a goal as a couple covers what to do when you fall behind.

Look ahead: car insurance of €420 is due in December, so €210 each. They also have December gifts coming and agree a rough limit.

One change: they'll add a small “home things” line of €30 a month so broken kettles stop being a surprise.

Total time: about 20 minutes, and the next month is planned.

Step 4: Add-ons every few months

Some things don't need a monthly look. Add them to every third check-in, or once a year:

  • Every three months: go through subscriptions, check whether your split still feels fair, and look at progress on bigger goals.
  • Once a year: review your budget as a whole, update incomes if they've changed, plan yearly costs like insurance, and set goals for the year ahead.
  • When life changes: a new job, a move, a baby or a career break is a good moment for a longer talk.

Our couples budget template is a good base for the yearly review.

Step 5: Keep it short and pleasant

  • Pick a nice setting. Coffee on a Sunday morning or a walk works better than a desk.
  • Stick to numbers you both see. It's much easier than going from memory.
  • No blame for last month. Look at what happened and adjust; don't relitigate every purchase.
  • Leave personal money alone. If you've agreed amounts for each of you to spend freely, they're not part of the check-in.
  • Skip a month if you need to, but book the next one straight away.

If one of you finds money talks stressful, keep the first few check-ins very short, ten minutes, and only cover spending and settling up. Add the rest once it feels normal.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Only talking when something goes wrong. Then every money talk feels like a problem.
  • No numbers ready. Without them, it turns into “I think I paid more”.
  • Too long. Two hours once a month is hard to keep up; twenty minutes isn't.
  • Too many changes at once. One change a month is enough.
