How to make money as a small influencer: 8 ways that work

You don't need a huge following to earn from your content. Here are 8 ways to make money as a small influencer, which ones work best for a small, engaged audience, and a simple 90-day plan to get started.

What counts as a small influencer

There's no official cut-off, but the industry usually talks about nano influencers (about 1,000 to 10,000 followers) and micro influencers (about 10,000 to 100,000). If you're in either group, you're "small" by marketing standards, and that's not a bad thing.

Small accounts often have something big accounts lose: a close, engaged audience. People comment, reply to stories and actually try what you recommend. Many brands know this, which is why smaller creators can earn real money long before they hit a big follower count.

The key is choosing ways of earning that reward trust and engagement, not just reach.

8 ways to make money as a small influencer

1. UGC for brands

With user-generated content (UGC), brands pay you to make photos or videos they use in their own ads and pages. They're paying for your content skills, not your audience, so follower count barely matters. It's one of the most accessible ways to start earning with a small account.

Start by: making three or four sample videos for products you already own, and putting them in a simple portfolio you can send to brands.

2. Affiliate links

Share products you genuinely use and earn a commission on sales through your link. Small audiences that trust you tend to click and buy more than large, passive ones.

Start by: adding affiliate links to the products people already ask you about, and creating one evergreen post or link-in-bio page that lists your favorites.

3. Paid brand collaborations

Many brands now run campaigns with lots of smaller creators instead of a few big ones. Rates are lower than for large accounts, but they add up, and they often lead to repeat work.

Start by: making a one-page media kit with your niche, audience, engagement and two or three examples of your best posts. Then pitch small brands you already love.

4. Gifted products that lead to paid work

Free products aren't income, and you shouldn't accept them for work you'd normally charge for. But a well-made gifted post can be a portfolio piece that wins paid deals later.

Start by: only accepting gifts from brands you'd actually recommend, and asking up front whether there's a paid option.

5. Digital products

Templates, presets, guides and planners sell well to engaged audiences, because they solve a specific problem your followers already have. You make them once and sell them again and again.

Start by: looking at your most-saved posts. The topic people save most is usually your first product.

6. Services built on your skills

The skills that grew your account are valuable on their own: content creation, editing, social media management or coaching in your niche. Local businesses, in particular, often need help and will pay for it.

Start by: offering one clear service with a fixed price, like "a month of content for your shop".

7. A small paid community

Even 50 members paying a few dollars a month is meaningful recurring income. Small creators often run better communities, because they can actually talk to everyone.

Start by: offering something specific, like a weekly check-in, a challenge or bonus content, rather than "access to me".

8. Your own app

If your audience comes to you for daily motivation, calm or encouragement, an app with your name on it can turn that daily habit into monthly subscriptions. With a white-label app, there's no building involved, so it's realistic even for small creators.

Start by: asking your audience whether they'd use a daily app from you. Our guide to white-label apps for influencers explains how they work and what to ask.

A simple 90-day plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: Pick your lane. Write one sentence about who you help and how. For example: "I help busy moms feel calmer with small daily habits." Everything you make should fit it.
  2. Weeks 3 to 4: Set up the basics. Create a link-in-bio page, a short media kit and a UGC sample or two. Add affiliate links to products you already recommend.
  3. Month 2: Start pitching. Contact five small brands a week with a short, personal message and one idea for them. Track who replies.
  4. Month 2: Ask your audience. Use a story poll or a question box to find out what they'd pay for: a template, a community, an app or something else.
  5. Month 3: Launch one product. Build the simplest version of the most-requested idea and mention it naturally in your content every week.
Track your engagement rate, not just followers. Brands and buyers care about how many people react to your posts, and a small account with strong engagement often outperforms a bigger one.

Mistakes small creators make

  • Waiting for a magic follower number. You can start earning with a small, engaged audience. Waiting just delays learning what works.
  • Working for free too long. Gifted products and "exposure" don't pay bills. Set a rate, even a small one, and stick to it.
  • Promoting everything. Every off-brand sponsorship spends trust. Say no to products you wouldn't use.
  • Relying on one income source. Mix at least two or three, like affiliate links, UGC and a product. Our guide to revenue streams for creators shows how to plan the mix.
  • Forgetting to disclose. Always label ads, gifts and affiliate links clearly. It's required in many countries, and it protects your audience's trust.

Frequently asked questions

Can you make money with 1,000 followers?

Yes. UGC, affiliate links, services and small digital products don't depend on a big audience. A small, engaged following that trusts you is often enough to start earning.

How do small influencers get paid by brands?

Through paid collaborations, UGC projects and affiliate programs. A short media kit and personal pitches to brands you already like are the usual way in.

What is UGC and why is it good for small creators?

UGC is content you make for a brand to use in its own ads and pages. Brands pay for your content skills, not your follower count, so it works well for small accounts.

How much do small influencers make?

It varies widely by niche, engagement and how many income streams you combine. Many start with a modest side income and grow it by adding a second or third stream.

What's the fastest way for a small influencer to start earning?

Affiliate links and UGC usually bring the first income fastest, because they need little setup. Products and communities take longer but can earn more over time.
