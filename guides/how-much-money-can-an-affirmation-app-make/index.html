How much money can an affirmation app make?

An affirmation app earns from subscribers, month after month. Here's a simple formula, a table from 50 to 2,000 subscribers, and how to estimate what your own audience could bring in.

Updated 2026-11-28

Short answer

It depends on subscribers, not downloads. Multiply subscribers by the monthly price and by 0.85 (what's left after Apple's 15% fee) to get net sales. At $7.99 a month, 200 subscribers bring in about $1,358 a month in net sales, 500 about $3,396 and 1,000 about $6,792. On acanalab's Partner plan the creator keeps 70% of that (about $951, $2,377 and $4,754 a month); on Pay once the creator keeps all of it. No one can honestly guarantee income: it depends on your audience and how you share the app.

The simple formula

Downloads don't pay. Subscribers do. To estimate what an affirmation app earns each month:

Subscribers × monthly price × 0.85 = net sales
The 0.85 is what's left after Apple's 15% fee for small developers. Your income is your share of net sales.

On acanalab's Partner plan your share is 70% of net sales. On Pay once it's 100%. If you build the app another way, your share is whatever is left after your own build and running costs.

From followers to subscribers

The number that matters is how many followers end up paying. It depends on how much your audience trusts you, how well the app fits what you share, and how often you mention it. You can make your own estimate with three numbers:

  1. Followers who see your launch posts. Your real reach, not your follower count.
  2. Share who download. Free apps are easy to try, so this is the biggest step.
  3. Share of downloads who subscribe. Usually a small part of downloads, which is normal for free apps with a subscription.

Multiply them, then use the formula above. Run it with a cautious guess and a hopeful one, and plan around the cautious one.

Worked example

Here's a made-up example, not a forecast. A self-care creator reaches 20,000 people with launch posts. 1,500 download the app over the first months, and 120 of them subscribe at $7.99 a month. Later posts and word of mouth bring in 30 more subscribers a month, while some cancel, so after a year the app has 350 subscribers.

  • Net sales: 350 × $7.99 × 0.85 ≈ $2,377 a month.
  • On Partner, the creator keeps about $1,664 a month.
  • On Pay once, about $2,377 a month.

What makes the number bigger

  • Fit. An affirmations app works best when it matches what your audience already comes to you for: encouragement, calm, confidence, self-love.
  • Sharing it again and again. One launch post isn't enough. Show the app in stories, mention your favorite affirmation of the week, share cards from it.
  • A daily habit. Reminders, widgets and streaks keep people opening the app, which keeps them subscribed.
  • Your voice. A few categories of affirmations written by you make the app feel personal, not generic.
  • A yearly option. Many people prefer a yearly plan at a lower monthly cost, and yearly subscribers tend to stay longer.

Frequently asked questions

How much do affirmation apps charge?

Many charge a few dollars a month or a yearly price, with a free version to try first. The examples here use $7.99 a month.

How many followers do I need for an affirmation app?

There's no fixed number. Engagement and fit matter more than size: a few thousand people who trust you can bring more subscribers than a large audience that doesn't.

When does Apple pay app earnings?

Apple pays developers monthly for the sales of each month, directly to the bank account in the developer's account.

Is an affirmation app passive income?

Partly. The app keeps earning from existing subscribers without new posts, but it grows faster when you keep sharing it.

Can an affirmation app lose money?

With acanalab Partner, your only fixed cost is Apple's $99 a year. With Pay once or a custom build, you pay upfront and need enough subscribers to earn it back.
