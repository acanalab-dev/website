Creators have five ways to get a branded app. Here's how they compare on cost, time, your work and who owns the app, and when each one makes sense.

Agency

An agency designs and builds an app just for you, with a team of designers, developers and testers. You get exactly what you ask for, and you pay for all of it: usually tens of thousands of dollars to build, then more for every update, fix and new iOS version. It makes sense for big brands with a unique idea, less so for testing whether your audience wants an app.

Freelancer

A freelance developer usually costs less than an agency, but you take on the project management: writing the brief, checking designs, testing builds and handling the App Store. Quality and availability vary, so check what happens when something breaks a year from now.

No-code app builder

A builder lets you put an app together yourself from templates, for a monthly fee. It's the cheapest way to build something custom, but you're the designer, tester and App Store submitter. Check before you start that the builder supports what keeps an affirmations app alive: Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, daily reminders, streaks and Apple subscriptions. Also check whose developer account the app is published under, and what happens if you stop paying the builder.

Course and community platform apps

If you already sell a course or membership on a platform that offers a branded app, that app can be a good fit for existing members. It's built around the platform's content, though, not around a daily affirmation habit, and it usually comes with the platform's ongoing fees.

When acanalab isn't the right choice

  • You need Android at launch. acanalab starts with iPhone. Android comes later, once the iPhone app does well and it makes sense for both sides.
  • You want a different kind of app. Today it's daily affirmations. If your audience would love something else, an agency or a builder may fit better, or write to us and we'll talk it through.
  • You need features unique to you. A custom build gives you full control, if you have the budget for it and for its upkeep.
  • You don't have an audience yet. An app grows when you share it. Build the audience first.

You do. The app is published under your own Apple Developer account, so the listing, the reviews and the subscribers are yours, and Apple pays you directly.

No. You don't build anything. acanalab builds, tests and publishes a finished affirmations app with your branding, and keeps it updated.

Not yet. It starts with iPhone, and builds Android once the iPhone app does well and it makes sense for both sides.
